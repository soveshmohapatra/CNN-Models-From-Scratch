The convolution operation is deceptively simple.

Slide a small kernel across an image. At each position, compute a weighted sum. Repeat for dozens of filters.

That's it. Yet this simple operation, when stacked into deep hierarchies, learns to detect everything from edges to object parts.

Part 2 of my CNN series is live, building the complete architecture in PyTorch:

✅ Custom Conv2D layer with learnable kernels
✅ MaxPool2D and AveragePool2D implementations
✅ LeNet-5, SimpleCNN, and DeepCNN architectures
✅ Reusable CNN blocks with BatchNorm

The key insight: weight sharing. The same filter slides across the entire image, detecting a specific feature regardless of location. This is translation equivariance in action.
